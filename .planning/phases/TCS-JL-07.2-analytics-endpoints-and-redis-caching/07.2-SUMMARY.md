# Phase 7.2 Plan 07.2-01: Analytics Endpoints & Redis Caching Summary

**Phase 7.2 put 7.1's read-only analytics service behind HTTP: five anonymous endpoints, a payload-level Redis cache behind the hourly warmup `config/celery.py` had already reserved, per-row `<5` suppression, and both wait-time baselines published side by side — closing the two decisions 7.1 left owed.**

## Performance

- **Endpoints (ANAL-01/ANAL-02):** `GET /api/v1/analytics/{overview,batches,hiring-types,regions}/` (04 §48–§51) plus `GET /api/v1/public/stats/` (04 §80), all `AllowAny` because 04 §6 lists landing-page information and public aggregate statistics under Anonymous. Thin `APIView`s over the service layer; `config/urls.py` includes the new `apps.analytics.urls` under the shared prefix, so §80's documented path resolves without a second URL module.
- **Caching (ANAL-05):** `apps/analytics/cache.py` caches the **assembled payload** per endpoint + filter signature through Django's cache framework (never a raw Redis client, per 6.2 D3), with `ANALYTICS_CACHE_TTL = 7200` read at call time. `analytics.tasks.warm_analytics_cache` refreshes the unfiltered default of all five endpoints and runs hourly on the `maintenance` queue; its name byte-matches the reserved beat entry and the settings route, asserted by test.
- **Attribution (ANAL-05):** every response — fresh, cached or suppressed — carries `data_source: "COMMUNITY_REPORTED"` and the non-affiliation disclaimer, plus a compute-time `generated_at` on non-suppressed payloads so a cache hit reports when its numbers were computed (D-06).
- **Per-row suppression (ANAL-04 strengthened):** `apply_row_floor` drops every below-floor row entirely — never zeroed, never labeled, never counted — and an all-below-floor response returns the 04 §53 payload (D-01/D-02/D-03). This closes 4.2's residual-risk vector, where a 200-candidate national slice could emit a batch row reporting 3.
- **Both wait-time baselines (ANAL-03):** `calculate_wait_time_metrics` (offer→JL, 7.1 D1's four-step chain) now names its baseline and reports per-source sample counts; `calculate_survey_wait_time_metrics` adds READINESS_SURVEY→JL, the requirement's literal wording and T7.7's. Each has its own sample size, its own `<5` floor and its own suppression, both exposed through the cached overview payload (D-13/D-14/D-16). ANAL-03 moves off Partial with no requirement text rewritten.
- **Throttling and validation (D-11/D-12):** an `analytics_reads` scope (120/min) guards the reads — including the cache-busting path where an anonymous caller walks region × hiring_type × batch to force uncached computation — and filters are whitelist-validated with a 400 that names the offending parameter instead of silently answering a typo with community-wide numbers.
- **Live Proof:** `drill_07_02.py` seeded 14 candidates into the real dev database and passed **19/19** checks through the real URLconf and cache — anonymous 200s, payload shapes, both baselines with different averages (survey 32.7d vs offer 53.0d), the per-row floor over HTTP, the four-key suppressed payload, three 400s, cache-hit stability, warmup refresh and restamping, the absent §52 route, no candidate identifier in any body, and a cleanup that restored the database to 21/21 candidates and 66/66 events and re-warmed the cache from that baseline.

## Decisions Made

All sixteen came from the discuss-phase session (`07.2-CONTEXT.md`); execution added these:

- **`throttle_scope` is declared on the view, not just the class.** DRF's `ScopedRateThrottle.allow_request` does `self.scope = getattr(view, 'throttle_scope', None)` and returns `True` — allow — when the view has none, so a class-level `scope` is inert. 7.2 sets both and asserts the bucket engages (429 after the rate is exceeded). This is also why **community's write throttle does nothing today** (Findings).
- **The region filter's charset is permissive** (`isalnum` plus ` -.'(),/&`, max 100 chars) rather than a vocabulary: 3.1 D3 makes `region` free text, and a whitelist built from regions that currently have candidates would 400 a real region with no data. Digits are allowed (`Sector 62`); safety comes from the key being a sha256 digest.
- **The warmup refreshes rather than fills.** Overwriting the hot keys is what bounds staleness to roughly the beat cadence; the TTL stays the backstop for a missed run.

## Findings

- **Community's write throttle is inert (NEW, HIGH).** `CommunityWriteRateThrottle` is attached to six community views and none of them declares `throttle_scope`, so DRF allows every request: post/comment/vote/lock/pin writes are unthrottled in fact. This is adjacent to, but distinct from, 05.2's F2 (post creation has no `throttle_classes` at all) — even the views that *have* the class get no rate limiting. `apps/notifications/throttles.py` is unaffected: it uses `UserRateThrottle`, where the class-level `scope` is authoritative. Recorded in STATE.md Pending Todos and Blockers.
- **Cached analytics are not invalidated by data changes.** A payload lives up to 2 hours and is refreshed hourly, so a manual data fix is not publicly visible until one of those fires. Designed (D-05), but operationally visible: the drill's first read was served the *previous* run's payload until it purged the `analytics:*` namespace before seeding. Any future live verification of these endpoints must purge first.
- **The drill earned its keep.** It failed four checks before the gates did, and three of those were bad assumptions rather than broken code: the dev database already had 2026 candidates (so the seeded "small batch" was not small), `/regions/` does not accept a `region` filter (so that 400 probe hit an ignored parameter and returned 200), and two `generated_at` stamps inside the same second compare equal. Each fix made the check deterministic instead of dependent on ambient data.
- **Deleted accounts are still counted publicly** (D-15, accepted): every published cohort includes anonymized-but-present profiles, because 4.2's F1 retention gap is unfixed. Recorded in STATE.md Blockers rather than silently changed here.
